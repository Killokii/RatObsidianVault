
To perform hematurgy, one must be in possession of two aspects: a Thorn and an organ called Prawia's Rena. Without these two, creating magical phenomena is nigh impossible—at least without the divine intervention of a Pijawka.

##### Now, what are Prawia's Renas?

Let us first discuss the more important aspect of hematurgy: Prawia's Rena, or Rena for short, is an organ located below the heart which is able to imbue one's blood with magical properties, as well as filter Prawia's blood into one's own. Without it, one's blood will not have enough power to be used for any acts of hematurgy—and worse, one will not be able to ingest the blood of Prawia without it. The issue is, though, that if one is not born a noble, they will not have a Rena in their body. This is why there is such a big gap in power between the Noble and the Lowly: a noble, unlike the Lowly, can use hematurgy straight out of the womb. It must be noted that, like with all things, there are exceptions to this. There are rare cases of a Lowly being born with a Rena, and vice versa. (To give an example, my crimson-armored friend is one of the few lowly I know born with a rena)

For the Lowly, the issue of the Rena has to be overcome with wit rather than force. Through a quite gruesome—and honestly quite dangerous—surgery, a Lowly can get a synthetic Rena embedded into their body. These synthetic Renas are, sadly, not a perfect substitute for an organic one, and as such require regular upkeep and outpoints somewhere on one's body to let out the huge amounts of heat they create. From personal experience, it is like having a little sun in your chest. Heat flashes and fevers come commonly with it inside one's body, and sometimes, when overused, it gets to the point that your whole body becomes red and lets off steam like an out-of-control locomotive. I've heard of cases of people being cooked alive from the inside out from faulty Renas and/or overuse of them. There have even been explosions from the inside out documented—quite nasty stuff. Though, these are the dangers the less fortunate have to endure to match natural-born capabilities.

This is why, although the Lowly make up the vast amount of the population, the amount of Lowly who can perform acts of hematurgy is quite small. Well, this and the fact that this method—on top of the dangers and cost of upkeep—still requires an egregious cost to be done or great connections to be acquired.

It must be noted that Renas (both synthetic and organic) can be consumed for an increase in power. It is even said that if a Lowly eats enough of these organs, they can grow their own.

### **Thorns and Needles**
All magical armaments are broadly categorized into two types: **Thorns** and **Needles**. Both require **blood** to activate—often a wielder’s own or blood offered through ritual or combat. These tools are not passive; they are hungry, living extensions of Prawia’s influence.

#### **Cold Thorns**

Forged from a metal that is unnaturally cold to the touch, Cold Thorns are brutal, focused weapons. Their strength lies in **specialization**—they excel at performing a single skill or function with exceptional precision and power. Those who wield Cold Thorns often undergo bodily modification or possess strengthened physiques, as these weapons demand resilience and focus. Their **area of effect is small**, but within that range, they are devastating.

#### **Warm Thorns**

In contrast, Warm Thorns are made from a metal that is always warm, almost alive. These are the tools of mages and scholars. Warm Thorns channel **standardized magic formulas** and boast a much **larger reach**, functioning more like conduits than weapons. Their design favors flexibility and consistency over raw power.

#### **Needles**

Needles are accessories—rings, amulets, piercings, or embedded shards—used to channel, regulate, or amplify blood magic. Like Thorns, they must be **fed blood** to function. Some act as magical reservoirs; others serve ritual purposes, bindings, or contracts. Many are deeply personal, bonded to their wearers through sacrifice or oath.
